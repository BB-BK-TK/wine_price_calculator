What is original vs. what is ours?
Original idea: winter rainfall + growing-season temperature − harvest rainfall. Prototype extension: apply a grape multiplier and simplified climate assumptions so the interaction works across more regions. The extension is exploratory and should not be attributed to Ashenfelter.
WHY THIS EXISTS The Freakonomics idea behind the calculator
⌄

This started with a wonderfully weird idea.

Economist Orley Ashenfelter challenged the wine world with a simple question: could you predict a Bordeaux vintage from weather data — without even tasting the wine? That idea, popularized for a broad audience through data-driven economics stories such as Freakonomics, became the starting point for this prototype.

The famous simplified Bordeaux Equation
Score = 12.145 + 0.00117 × Winter Rain + 0.0614 × Growing-Season Temp − 0.00386 × Harvest Rain
🌧️ Winter rain +More stored water can help vines through the growing season.
☀️ Growing heat +Warmer growing seasons can help grapes ripen more fully.
🌦️ Harvest rain −More rain near harvest can dilute fruit and increase risk.

Ashenfelter's original research focused on mature red Bordeaux and auction-price patterns. This prototype extends the idea beyond Bordeaux with simplified region and grape assumptions, so treat it as an experiment — not a live retail quote.
